## CLINC150: where the cascade loses

R3 makes 692 errors on 5500 items; **474 (68%) are out-of-scope requests it answered with an in-scope intent**.

| Scope | Routing ended at | Answered | Errors |
|---|---|---|---|
| out of scope | Tier 3 | yes | 384 |
| in scope | Tier 3 | yes | 107 |
| out of scope | Tier 4 | yes | 90 |
| in scope | Tier 4 | no (abstained) | 87 |
| in scope | Tier 4 | yes | 24 |

R1 (the encoder alone) is right where R3 is wrong on 261 items, and the reverse on 157. The mechanism is the Tier-3 threshold: R3 accepts Tier 3 at confidence ≥ 0.601 (the cheapest point on val that meets the accuracy floor, ADR-019/021), while R1 abstains below 0.782. 149 out-of-scope test requests fall in that band and R3 answers them; R1 does not.
Out-of-scope requests are 11.8% of val but 18.2% of test (the official test keeps its 1000 OOS items), so a threshold chosen for cost on val under-weights them: a shift in the out-of-scope rate between val and test, not a calibration failure (E2: Tier-3 ECE 0.008 after T).

## Kestrel-Intents-v0 (synthetic): every R3 error

R3 makes 8 errors on 252 items (en 7, hi-en 1); 6 of them are Tier-1 rule matches (the rules were accepted at confidence 1.0, their precision on cal).

**6 are compound requests accepted as one intent below Tier 4.** The multi-intent signal only knows and, then, aur, phir, fir (ADR-017), so requests joined by "also" or ", now" were not flagged, and a single-intent rule took them. This is why the rules cost accuracy on test (E3: {4} 1.000 vs {1,4} 0.976). They come from the families cmp-08, cmp-09; all 24 val compounds contain a signal word (0 unflagged), so tuning could not see the gap. A wider signal is a post-hoc change: a new, exploratory version.
